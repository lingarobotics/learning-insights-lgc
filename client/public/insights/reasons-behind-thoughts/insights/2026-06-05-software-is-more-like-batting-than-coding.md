# Reasons Behind Thoughts — Software Engineering Is More Like Batting Than Coding


---

# Why This Post Exists

While reflecting on cricket and software engineering, I noticed an unexpected similarity.

Most people associate cricket with batting.

Most people associate software engineering with coding.

Both observations are technically correct.

Neither captures what actually makes someone effective.

This post records the engineering principles that emerged from that comparison.

---

# The Core Narrative Of The Entire Post

The progression follows a shift in perspective.

Not:

* coding as the primary engineering skill

But:

* decision-making under uncertainty.

The flow is:

1. Engineering is more than implementation.
2. Prediction often matters more than reaction.
3. Systems continuously introduce uncertainty.
4. Architecture reflects assumptions.
5. Observability reveals hidden system behavior.
6. Engineering rewards understanding over execution.

Each realization builds upon the previous one.

---

# Coding Is Only One Part Of Engineering

Programming is an important skill.

But writing code is only one activity within software engineering.

Requirements.

Architecture.

Communication.

Trade-offs.

Debugging.

Deployment.

Maintenance.

All of these exist before and after writing code.

The more I think about engineering, the less I associate it solely with implementation.

---

# Prediction Creates Better Decisions

One observation from cricket stood out.

Elite batsmen rarely rely on reaction alone.

They continuously observe patterns.

Previous deliveries.

Field placement.

Bowler behavior.

Small pieces of information improve future decisions.

Engineering feels similar.

Good engineers don't simply react to problems.

They build mental models that help them anticipate them.

---

# Software Systems Rarely Remain Stable

One beginner realization I had was expecting software requirements to remain mostly fixed.

The more I learned, the more I realized software behaves differently.

Requirements evolve.

Business priorities change.

Dependencies change.

Technology changes.

Engineering isn't about working in certainty.

It's about making good decisions despite uncertainty.

---

# Architecture Represents Assumptions

Another realization was that architecture isn't simply about diagrams or components.

Every architectural decision assumes something about the future.

Expected scale.

Expected traffic.

Expected reliability.

Expected growth.

Good architecture isn't predicting the future perfectly.

It's making reasonable decisions based on current understanding while accepting that assumptions may eventually change.

---

# Observability Is Part Of Understanding Systems

Production systems continuously generate information.

Logs.

Metrics.

Monitoring.

Tracing.

Alerts.

Initially, they appeared to be operational tools.

Now I see them differently.

They are mechanisms that help engineers understand what the system is actually doing instead of what they assume it is doing.

---

# Engineering Is Continuous Decision-Making

The more I reflected on cricket, the more another similarity became obvious.

Neither great batsmen nor great engineers operate with complete information.

Both continuously evaluate changing situations.

Every new piece of information influences the next decision.

Execution matters.

Decision quality matters even more.

---

# Systems Thinking Connects Everything

Individual technologies often appear isolated while learning.

Programming.

Databases.

Networking.

Cloud.

Artificial Intelligence.

Real engineering connects them into one system.

Understanding relationships between components often becomes more valuable than understanding components independently.

---

# Beginner Insight

One realization I noted for myself is that software engineering isn't simply becoming better at writing code.

It is gradually becoming better at understanding systems, recognizing patterns, questioning assumptions, and making informed decisions under changing conditions.

Coding remains important.

But coding alone doesn't define engineering.

---

# Final Understanding

Watching cricket made me realize something unexpected.

The best performers often appear calm because they have already processed much of the situation before acting.

The same seems true in software engineering.

Great engineers don't only build systems.

They understand them.

The deeper my learning progresses, the more software engineering appears to be a discipline of reasoning, prediction, and systems thinking rather than programming alone.
